# Literature map and positioning audit

This note records the sources used to design the model and separates established
mechanisms from the paper's proposed synthesis. It is not a substitute for a
systematic literature review, but it makes the paper's intellectual provenance
auditable.

## Core mechanisms

| Source | Mechanism used in the paper | Role here |
|---|---|---|
| Stiglitz and Weiss (1981), *Credit Rationing in Markets with Imperfect Information* | A loan rate need not clear the credit market; screening and incentive effects can generate quantity rationing. | Justifies modeling bank credit as a ceiling rather than assuming that every firm borrows freely at one price. |
| Holmström and Tirole (1997), *Financial Intermediation, Loanable Funds, and the Real Sector* | Internal net worth and intermediary capital determine firms' financing capacity and real investment. | Motivates the pledgeability/net-worth interpretation of the bank ceiling. |
| Bernanke, Gertler, and Gilchrist (1999), *The Financial Accelerator in a Quantitative Business Cycle Framework* | External-finance premia and balance sheets amplify shocks. | Connects the static comparative statics to a possible dynamic financial-accelerator extension. |
| Kiyotaki and Moore (1997), *Credit Cycles* | Collateral constraints couple asset values, borrowing, and production dynamically. | Motivates making the credit ceiling endogenous in later versions. |
| Petersen and Rajan (1994), *The Benefits of Lending Relationships* | Lending relationships can improve small firms' access to finance. | Supports cross-firm heterogeneity in bank dependence and financing access. |
| Gertler and Gilchrist (1994), *Monetary Policy, Business Cycles, and the Behavior of Small Manufacturing Firms* | Small, financially constrained firms respond differently to monetary contractions. | Supports heterogeneous transmission by firm type. |
| Gilchrist and Zakrajšek (2012), *Credit Spreads and Business Cycle Fluctuations* | Corporate-bond spreads contain information about economic activity, including an excess-bond-premium component. | Motivates the price-of-credit predictor and the market-finance margin. |
| Müller and Verner (2024), *Credit Allocation and Macroeconomic Fluctuations* | The closest sectoral paper: credit allocation toward non-tradables versus tradables predicts different macro outcomes; the paper discusses smaller, more constrained non-tradable firms and includes bond-issuance robustness. | Rules out claiming novelty for the sector split or financing heterogeneity. The remaining claim must be limited to the explicit quantity-versus-price comparative-static ranking. |
| Gilchrist, Sim, and Zakrajsek (2014), NBER working paper | Models the joint dynamics of investment and credit spreads. | Rules out claiming that linking the spread to investment dynamics is itself new. |

## Forecasting and Peruvian evidence

| Source | Relevance |
|---|---|
| Stock and Watson (2003), *Forecasting Output and Inflation: The Role of Asset Prices* | Benchmark for asking whether financial variables add out-of-sample predictive content. |
| Faust et al. (2013), *Credit Spreads as Predictors of Real-Time Economic Activity* | Shows how credit spreads can be evaluated as real-time predictors while accounting for model uncertainty. |
| Lahura (2011), BCRP Working Paper 2011-018 | Documents a relationship between bank credit and economic activity in Peru and supplies local institutional motivation. |
| Butrón and Pozo (2025), BCRP Working Paper 2025-011 | Recent Peruvian evidence on credit conditions and real activity; useful for updating the local discussion. |
| Fernández and Chávez (2026), undergraduate thesis draft supplied by the authors | Supplies preliminary qualitative evidence that credit quantity may be more informative for nontradables at relatively short horizons, while a credit-price spread may be more informative for tradables over a longer horizon. Exact peaks are not treated as settled results. |

## Proposed contribution and claims discipline

The paper does **not** claim to have invented credit rationing, the financial
accelerator, heterogeneous financing access, or sectoral credit transmission.
Its proposed contribution is narrower: a tractable piecewise model in which the
same firm problem produces a quantity-sensitive kink regime and a
price-sensitive market-finance regime, and in which sectoral composition maps
those margins into the empirical quantity/nontradables and price/tradables
pattern.

The literature audit found close antecedents for every broad ingredient. The
remaining provisional contribution is the explicit theorem ranking sectoral
output sensitivity to credit quantity versus a common financing price under a
capped bank tranche and an outside-finance margin. The current draft therefore
uses language such as "we propose" and "the model rationalizes," never "this
is the first paper."
